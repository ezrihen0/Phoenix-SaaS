# Feature Work Order
# The 10 Commandments of Product Management

This checklist is for the product owner before involving AI, developers, or execution work.

The goal is to make sure every feature is clear, valuable, controlled, testable, and safe to release.

---

## 1. Why — Why am I building this?

I must know why this matters right now.

This feature must help the business in at least one clear way: make more money, save time, prevent mistakes, improve customer service, or give me better control.

---

## 2. Priority — Is this truly first?

I check whether this is actually urgent and important, or just an idea that feels exciting in the moment.

If it does not affect sales, operations, customers, or system stability, it is not the first priority.

---

## 3. Define — What exactly are we building?

I define the feature in one simple business sentence.

If I cannot explain it in one sentence, I am not ready to start.

---

## 4. Scope — What is included and what is not included?

I set clear boundaries: what we are doing now, what we are not doing now, and what must not be touched.

This prevents the work from expanding into a mess.

---

## 5. Map — What parts are connected to this feature?

I identify the parts connected to the feature: screens, buttons, data, customers, employees, payments, reports, or automations.

The goal is to understand the work area before touching anything.

---

## 6. Risk — What can break?

I ask in advance what could be affected by this change.

This may include the website, CRM, customers, data, payments, login, or an existing business workflow.

---

## 7. Approve — What exactly am I approving?

I do not approve vague work like “work on this.”

I approve a clear work list: what will be done, what will not be done, and what result I expect to see.

---

## 8. Build — Small and clean execution

I require a small, focused execution slice.

No “while we are here” changes, no unrelated improvements, and no opening extra work fronts during the task.

---

## 9. Test — How do I know it works?

I test it like a real user.

The page must open, the buttons must work, the data must save, and the change must not break something else.

---

## 10. Release & Review — Deploy and check after

I release only when there is a clear rollback point.

After release, I check the real production site. Once it works, I write down what I learned and what the next step is.

---

## Prime Rule

Think freely, but approve only written decisions.

Before execution, every feature must have a short Feature Card:

```text
Feature:
Why now:
Priority:
Scope:
Done when:
```
