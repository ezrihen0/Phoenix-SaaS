### Professional / Technician Draft

**Residential sectional door system (typical):** Door sections (panels), horizontal tracks, vertical tracks, rollers, hinges, struts (if present), bottom fixture/brackets, lifting cables, counterbalance system (extension or torsion spring assembly), center bearing plate (torsion systems), drums (torsion), wall/ceiling brackets, weather seal, photo-eye safety sensors, wall control, remote, and optional battery backup or smart features on the operator.

**Operator (if present):** Rail/trolley, carriage, chain/belt/screw drive, motor unit, limits/force settings (manufacturer-specific), emergency release, and safety reversal features per UL 325 / manufacturer requirements.

**Diagnostic intake (field):**
- Door type: sectional (most common residential), one-piece tilt-up, or other
- Symptom timing: first cycle of day, intermittent, constant, after storm/wind, after manual release use
- Balance check concept: door should move smoothly without binding; balance assessment is a trained task—do not coach customers through balance tests that require releasing tension or standing under a partially open door
- Observe: visible cable fraying, gap in spring coils, bent track, roller off track, damaged bottom bracket area, sensor alignment/obstruction, opener lights/flash codes (per manufacturer manual)
- Ask whether customer used emergency release; if door was open when released, note fall-risk per manufacturer warnings
- Photo request: overall door, both sides of bottom area (from safe distance), sensors, operator label/model, damage area

**High-risk triggers (schedule trained technician; do not DIY coach):**
- Broken or visibly damaged spring(s)
- Frayed, slack, or broken lifting cable(s)
- Door off track or severely crooked in opening
- Door will not stay closed or slams shut
- Loud bang reported (possible spring failure)
- Customer under partially open door or attempting spring/cable/bracket work
- Opener runs but door does not move, or moves unevenly with grinding/binding

**Service opportunities (professional assessment required):**
- Preventive maintenance: lubrication and maintenance points per manufacturer guidance and company policy, hardware inspection, balance check by trained tech
- Safety sensor alignment/cleaning
- Weather seal replacement
- Roller/hinge wear
- Opener travel/force evaluation when door is confirmed balanced
- Full spring/cable replacement when worn or failed

### Owner / Admin Draft

Use this candidate to standardize **what your team captures on a residential service call** and **when to escalate to a qualified garage door technician**—not to replace manufacturer manuals or technician judgment.

**Business rules:**
- Dispatch should flag spring/cable/off-track/entrapment issues as **priority safety**, not “opener-only” jobs unless a qualified tech confirms otherwise on site.
- Do not promise repair outcomes, part compatibility, or warranty coverage without verification.
- Marketing copy must not imply homeowners can adjust springs, cables, or brackets.

### Dispatcher-Safe Draft

**Opening script (residential):**
“We’ll ask a few questions so we can send the right technician and parts. Garage doors use high-tension springs and cables—if anything looks broken or the door is crooked, please stay clear of the door path and don’t try to force it.”

**Intake checklist:**
1. **Location & access:** address, gate code, pets, vehicles in bay
2. **Door type:** sectional / one-piece / unsure
3. **Main problem (pick one primary):** won’t open, won’t close, opens partway, reverses immediately, loud noise, crooked/off track, opener runs door doesn’t move, remote/wall button issue, sensor issue, damaged panel, weather-related
4. **Operator present?** yes/no/unsure; brand/model if visible
5. **Safety screen (if yes → escalate priority):**
   - Visible broken spring or cable?
   - Door hanging crooked or off track?
   - Customer tried to adjust springs/cables/brackets?
   - Anyone injured or door fell?
6. **Timing:** when started, getting worse, weather events
7. **Photos:** request wide shot + bottom corners + sensors + operator label (customer stays clear)
8. **Scheduling note:** “high-tension / safety” vs “standard service” vs “opener diagnostic”

**Do not tell customer:** how many spring turns, how to rewind springs, how to re-seat cables, or how to force a binding door.

### Customer-Safe Draft

**Simple explanation (non-DIY):**
Your garage door is a large moving system with springs and cables that balance the weight of the door. If the door looks crooked, makes a loud bang, won’t stay open, or you see a broken spring or cable, **stop using the door** and contact a professional garage door company. Please keep people and vehicles away from the door path.

**What you can check safely (if comfortable):**
- Is something blocking the door path or blocking the small safety sensors near the floor?
- Did the remote need new batteries? Is the wall button working?
- Is the opener plugged in or on a tripped breaker? (only if safe to access)

**What not to do:**
- Do not try to fix, loosen, or adjust springs, cables, bottom brackets, or pulleys.
- Do not pull a hanging door back onto the track.
- Do not stand or walk under a partially open door that is not moving normally.

### Public-Safe Draft

**Marketing/education (high level):**
Residential garage doors should be installed, maintained, and repaired by trained door professionals. Modern systems include safety features such as photo-eye sensors and auto-reverse on openers. If your door is noisy, unbalanced, or unsafe, schedule professional service—do not attempt spring or cable repairs yourself.

## AI-Safe Draft

What the AI may say **if this becomes approved** and only on **allowed surfaces/audiences**:

- Describe common residential components (sections, tracks, rollers, cables, springs, operator, sensors) at a high level.
- Help dispatchers/technicians structure intake questions (symptoms, timing, safety screen, photos).
- Explain that springs and cables are under high tension and repairs require a trained garage door technician.
- Suggest stopping use of the door when crooked, broken spring/cable suspected, or door slams/falls.
- Offer customer-safe checks: path obstruction, sensor blockage, remote batteries, power—without mechanical repair steps.
- Recommend professional service categories: maintenance, sensor service, roller/hinge wear, opener diagnostic **after** door safety confirmed by a qualified tech.
- Use neutral report wording (see Audience drafts).

## AI Must Not Say

- Exact torsion spring turn counts, winding sequences, or “quarter-turn” adjustment instructions.
- Step-by-step instructions to repair or re-tension springs, cables, bottom brackets, or drums.
- Instructions to pull a door back on track or force a binding door.
- That permits are/are not required for a given city (not in scope).
- That a DIY homeowner can balance a door by adjusting springs.
- Brand-specific force/limit settings values without the exact manufacturer manual for that model.
- Definitive diagnosis (“your spring is broken”) without qualified inspection—use likelihood + escalate.
- Universal claims that all residential doors share identical parts or procedures.

## Escalation Rule

Escalate to **trained garage door technician** (human) when any of the following apply:

- Suspected broken spring or cable (visible damage, loud bang, door won’t lift, slack cable)
- Door off track, severely crooked, or binding
- Door does not stay open/closed as expected (balance concern)
- Customer or public user asks how to fix springs/cables/brackets
- Entrapment risk: auto-reverse fails, sensors missing/misaligned (per manufacturer/UL requirements), door closes on obstruction
- Injury, property damage, or door fell

Escalate to **manufacturer documentation** for opener-specific flash codes, limits, force settings, and entrapment device requirements.

Escalate to **expert reviewer / owner approval** before promoting any safety-sensitive statement to approved knowledge.
