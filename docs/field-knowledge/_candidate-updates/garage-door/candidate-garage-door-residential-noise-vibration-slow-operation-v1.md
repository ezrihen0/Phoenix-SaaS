### Professional / Technician Draft

**Noise / vibration / slow operation baseline overview**

Customer complaints: “sounds bad,” squeaking, grinding, rattling, popping, scraping, shaking, slow travel, hesitation, jerky movement, noisy first cycle of the day, opener sounds like it is straining.

**Core principle:** Noise **may** reflect wear/tune-up opportunities (rollers, hinges, track debris, seal drag, opener rail)—but **must not** be treated as harmless without safety screening. Loud **bang**, **heavy door**, **crooked travel**, or **visible spring/cable** issues are **high-risk**, not “noise tune-up.”

**Symptom buckets (observational—not definitive diagnosis from sound alone)**

| Bucket | Possible focus areas | Escalate if… |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| Roller wear / dry rollers | Rollers, stems, track contact | Binding, off-track roller |
| Hinge wear / loose hardware | Hinges, fasteners (non-tension) | Cracked hinge, door sag |
| Track debris / rubbing | Track cleanliness, alignment visual | Bent track, scraping with bind |
| Loose non-tension fasteners | Brackets, struts (per SOP) | Near cable/spring zone |
| Panel/section movement | Hinges, alignment | Crooked door |
| Weather seal / threshold drag | Bottom seal, threshold (R5) | Reverses near floor (R4) |
| Opener rail/trolley/motor | Rail, chain/belt, trolley (R4) | Strains, hums, door barely moves |
| Door imbalance concern | Spring/cable visual, travel | Loud bang, heavy, won’t stay |
| Spring/cable concern | Visual only | Any damage → high-risk |
| Off-track/binding | Track, rollers, crooked | Always high-risk |
| Climate stiffness | **R3** cold first cycle, humidity | Forced operation |

**Field guidance**

1. Safety screen (R1): bang, crooked, binding, cable/spring, injury.
2. Observe **one full cycle** only if path clear and door appears normal.
3. Localize sound: left/right track, hinges, opener rail/motor, bottom seal, cable/spring area (stay clear).
4. Document neutrally: “customer reports grinding”; “rattle at right track”; “slow travel observed.”
5. Inspect rollers, hinges, tracks, fasteners, seals, opener arm/rail/trolley visually.
6. Maintenance per **R2** + company policy—**not** springs/cables/bottom brackets.
7. **Do not** use opener force/limit as first-line fix; use **manufacturer manual** (R4).
8. **Do not** adjust springs/cables.

### Owner / Admin Draft

**Service categories**

- Noise diagnostic
- Tune-up package (R2)
- Roller replacement
- Hinge / hardware service
- Opener diagnostic (R4)
- Track / panel assessment
- Weather seal drag assessment (R5)
- High-risk spring/cable/off-track service (separate SKU)

**Required media:** full cycle video if safe; tracks/rollers; hinges; opener label; bottom seal; damage areas.

**QA rules**

- Do not close as **lubrication-only** if high-risk flags present.
- Do not **promise** noise elimination without inspection.
- Do not sell **opener replacement** before door mechanical safety assessed.
- Dispatch: **loud bang + heavy door** ≠ “noise tune-up” booking.

**Marketing:** Avoid “lubrication fixes all noise”; avoid DIY spring messaging.

### Dispatcher-Safe Draft

**Noise intake**

1. Sound type: squeak / grind / bang / pop / scrape / rattle / vibration / slow / jerky?
2. When: opening / closing / first cycle / cold morning / after repair / after impact?
3. Movement: smooth vs jerk/shake? One side rubbing?
4. **Safety screen:**
   - Loud bang?
   - Door heavy, drops, or slams?
   - Crooked or off track?
   - Opener runs but door barely moves?
   - Visible cable/spring damage?
   - Reverses or stops?
   - Injury/damage?
5. Opener struggling sound?
6. Safe video/photo from **away from door path** if door appears to move normally.

**Routing**

- Bang/heavy/crooked/cable/spring → **mechanical safety** priority.
- Squeak/rattle, normal movement → noise diagnostic / tune-up.
- Opener strain only → opener diagnostic (R4) after mechanical screen.

### Customer-Safe Draft

Some garage door noise comes from worn rollers, hinges, tracks, seals, or opener parts. A professional tune-up or inspection can identify the cause.

**Stop using the door and schedule service if you hear a loud bang**, the door moves crookedly, shakes badly, feels very heavy, drops or slams, or you see damaged cables or springs. Keep people and vehicles away from the door path.

You may record a **short video from a safe distance** only if the door seems to move normally and none of the warning signs above are present.

**Do not** lubricate or adjust springs, cables, or bottom brackets. **Do not** turn up opener settings or force a sticking door.

### Public-Safe Draft

A noisy garage door often signals wear or poor operation. Professional inspection can address rollers, hinges, tracks, seals, or opener components. A loud bang, a crooked door, a very heavy door, or visible spring or cable damage requires trained professional service—not DIY repair.

## AI Must Not Say

- All noisy doors just need lubrication.
- Customer should lubricate or adjust **springs/cables**.
- Tighten **bottom brackets** or cable fixtures.
- Increase opener **force/limits** for slow or noisy door.
- Force door through binding or grinding.
- **Exact failure diagnosis** from sound description alone.
- **Guarantee** complete noise elimination.
- Ignore loud bang, heavy door, or crooked movement.
- Invent **flash codes** or force/limit values without manual.
- Jurisdiction/code/AHJ claims.

## Escalation Rule

Escalate to **trained garage door technician** when:

- Loud bang
- Door heavy, drops, slams, or won’t stay open/closed
- Crooked, off-track, or binding
- Visible spring/cable damage
- Grinding/scraping with **uneven** travel
- Opener strains but door barely moves
- Entrapment/safety reverse failure
- Injury or property damage
- Customer asks for spring/cable/bottom bracket adjustment
- Customer asks to increase opener force/limits

Escalate to **manufacturer documentation** for opener motor/rail/trolley, flash codes, travel/force/limit, battery backup, smart features.
