### Professional / Technician Draft

#### 1. Commercial climate/environment overview (modifier-only)

- Climate/environment **modifies inspection focus** and documentation priorities.
- It does **not** replace door type identification (C2) or door-family baselines (C3, C4) or control/safety device baseline (C5) or PM program baseline (C6).
- Environment may affect doors differently based on door type, exposure, usage/cycles, site conditions, and maintenance history.
- Use cautious language: **“may contribute,” “can be associated with,” “inspection focus,” “possible factor.”** Avoid absolute causation.
- Do not claim city-specific requirements, code obligations, or compliance outcomes.

#### 2. Climate/environment groups (inspection modifiers, not diagnoses)

**A. Cold / freeze-thaw / snow / ice** (examples: Calgary/Edmonton/Winnipeg/Minnesota-type)

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Frozen bottom edge/seal/threshold area
  - Snow/ice blocking travel at floor line
  - Seasonal reports of stiff movement or cold-weather noise
  - Ice at guides/tracks/opening edges (where visible)
  - Operator strain reports in cold weather (document reported behavior; do not diagnose internals)
  - Photo-eye/safety device obstruction from snow/ice where applicable
- Safety boundary:
  - Do not force frozen/jammed doors.
  - If forced and now crooked/off-track/off-guide → **high-risk escalation**.

**B. Salt / de-icer exposure** (examples: loading bays, parking garages, winter road salt near doors)

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Visible corrosion on bottom hardware, guides/tracks, bottom bar, fasteners, section/slat lower areas
  - Higher attention to lower door area and floor line where salt accumulates
  - Salt/de-icer may accelerate visible corrosion
- Safety boundary:
  - Springs/cables/counterbalance areas are **high-risk visual-only**.
  - Do not treat corrosion near high-risk components as cosmetic-only without inspection.

**C. Hot / humid climate**

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Corrosion risk and condensation concerns
  - Seal deterioration (softening, damage, gaps) as observation
  - Sensor lens contamination/fog/condensation as possible focus
  - Operator heat/humidity concerns are manufacturer-specific (manual/source required for any claims)
- Safety boundary:
  - Do not translate humidity into definitive failure cause.

**D. Coastal salt-air** (examples: coastal Florida, coastal California; coastal exposure differs from inland cold climates)

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Accelerated visible corrosion potential on exposed hardware, guides, bottom bar, fasteners, panels/slats
  - Operator/safety device exposure as documentation item (enclosure/rating claims require manual/source)
- Safety boundary:
  - No exact lifespan claims or corrosion-prevention guarantees.
  - Springs/cables/counterbalance are **high-risk visual-only**.

**E. Dry / dusty** (examples: interior dry sites, dusty warehouses, industrial yards)

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Dust/debris accumulation around tracks/guides and sensor locations (observation only)
  - Dirty photo-eyes/safety device areas as inspection focus (no cleaning instructions here)
  - Seal drying/cracking (observation)
  - Operator/control enclosure contamination is manufacturer-specific (manual/source required for claims)
- Safety boundary:
  - Do not give internal cleaning, electrical, or cover-removal instructions.

**F. High-wind / storm exposure**

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Alignment issues, guide/track integrity, panel/slat/curtain damage, bottom bar damage
  - Impact from debris and storm events (document reported event)
- Safety boundary:
  - No wind-rating or code claims without approved source/AHJ/manufacturer review.

**G. Wet industrial / washdown / corrosive process environment** (examples: car wash, food washdown areas, chemical exposure zones)

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Wet exposure and visible corrosion/deterioration indicators
  - Operator/control/safety device exposure as manufacturer/manual review item
- Safety boundary:
  - Do not provide chemical compatibility claims without sources.

**H. Cold-storage / temperature differential**

- Possible inspection focus:
  - Condensation/icing and temperature differential as documentation context
  - Specialty/high-speed/freezer door systems are out of scope; route to expert/future topic
- Safety boundary:
  - Do not apply standard door assumptions to specialty cold-storage doors without review.

#### 3. Door-family climate considerations (high-level)

- **Commercial sectional**: panels/sections, tracks, rollers/hinges, bottom seal, operator/safety devices, corrosion/ice/impact exposure → route to C3.
- **Rolling steel**: curtain/slats, guides, bottom bar, hood/barrel area, chain hoist, operator/safety devices, corrosion/ice/debris exposure → route to C4.
- **Operators/controls/safety devices**: photo-eyes, safety edge, wall station, access controls, operator exposure → route to C5.
- **PM programs**: environment may influence PM focus and company-defined frequency → route to C6.
- **Fire/high-speed/specialty**: route to expert/future topics; C7 does not define compliance.

#### 4. Dispatcher intake for climate/environment calls (modifier intake)

- What city/region is the site in?
- What is the site type: warehouse, parking garage, loading dock, shop, storefront, cold storage, car wash, industrial?
- Is the opening exposed to weather or inside?
- Any snow/ice at the threshold/opening?
- Any salt/de-icer exposure near the door?
- Any visible rust/corrosion?
- Any water, condensation, or washdown exposure?
- Any dust/debris around tracks/guides/sensors?
- Any wind/storm/impact event?
- Door type: sectional / rolling steel / grille / counter / high-speed / fire-rated / unknown?
- Is the door/curtain stuck, jammed, crooked, off-track/off-guide, or moving abnormally?
- Does the operator run?
- Any safety edge/photo-eye concern?
- Is the business unable to open/close/secure?
- Can customer send safe photos: full opening, threshold, bottom corners/bottom bar, tracks/guides, operator, safety devices, labels/nameplates, visible corrosion/ice/damage?

**Dispatcher must not**

- Tell the customer to force a frozen/jammed door
- Tell the customer to chip/pry near high-risk hardware
- Tell the customer to bypass safety devices
- Give cleaning/chemical/electrical instructions
- Promise a climate-specific fix or guarantee

#### 5. Technician field guidance (professional-only boundary)

- Confirm door family first using C2 → route to C3/C4/C5 as needed.
- Document site environment:
  - exposed / interior
  - weather exposure
  - salt/de-icer exposure
  - washdown/wet exposure
  - dusty/debris exposure
  - cold storage / temperature differential
  - coastal/corrosive exposure
- Use **reported vs observed** language.
- Separate climate/environment observation from final diagnosis.
- Identify **high-risk overrides** (crooked/off-track/off-guide, cable/spring/counterbalance concern, impact, safety device concern/bypass, electrical concern).
- Link findings to PM focus and quote-review flags without guarantees (route to C6).
- Route manufacturer/environmental ratings, corrosion-resistant parts, operator enclosures, specialty doors, and fire/compliance questions to manual/expert review.
- Do not provide repair, adjustment, programming, wiring, bypass, force/limit/travel, or cleaning chemical procedures in C7.

### Dispatcher-Safe Draft

- Climate/environment context helps with **routing and documentation**, but does not replace door type identification or safety screening.
- Ask for site context and safe photos (threshold, bottom area, guides/tracks, operator/safety devices, labels).
- Do not advise forcing frozen/jammed doors or bypassing safety devices.
- Route door-family details to C3/C4/C5 and PM planning to C6.

### Customer-Safe Draft

- Weather and site conditions can **contribute** to wear, corrosion, sticking, sensor issues, or seasonal operation changes.
- A trained commercial door technician needs to inspect the specific door, site exposure, and safety devices.
- Do not force a frozen, jammed, crooked, off-track/off-guide, or damaged door.
- Do not bypass safety devices.
- Do not chip/pry near cables, bottom brackets, guides, bottom bar, springs, or operator components.
- Photos from a safe distance can help with scheduling and routing.
- Climate/environment-focused maintenance may reduce risk and improve documentation, but cannot guarantee no future failure.
- Final recommendations and pricing follow inspection and quote review.

### Public-Safe Draft

Commercial doors exposed to snow, salt, humidity, dust, wind, or industrial conditions may need different inspection focus. Professional inspection and PM planning should consider site conditions. No DIY repair, bypass, electrical, or compliance instructions. Fire-rated or specialty doors may require qualified review.

## Escalation Rule

Escalate to trained commercial door technician / owner-admin / manufacturer docs / expert/AHJ review when:

- Door is frozen, jammed, crooked, off-track/off-guide, impacted, or unsafe
- Customer forced the door and it now moves abnormally
- Counterbalance/spring/cable/curtain/guide/bottom bar concern exists
- Operator/safety device issue exists or bypass is requested/reported
- Significant corrosion near high-risk components is reported/visible
- Wet/washdown/corrosive environment affects operator/control/safety device
- Fire-rated/high-speed/specialty/cold-storage door involved
- Customer asks for compliance/certification statements
- Customer requests exact PM frequency without policy/manual
- Customer requests price/contract/warranty terms without business systems
- Injury/property damage
- Door type/nameplate/site context missing
