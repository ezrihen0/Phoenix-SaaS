### Professional / Technician Draft

**Core principle:** Climate **modifies** the R2 tune-up baseline (`candidate-garage-door-residential-tune-up-baseline-v1.md`). Complete the full R2 checklist first, then apply the relevant climate modifier below. Climate context **may contribute** to symptoms; it does not replace mechanical diagnosis or safety escalation.

**Field note language:** Use observational phrasing: “may be associated with,” “inspection focus,” “customer reported after freeze,” not definitive causation.

---

#### 1. Cold / freeze-thaw climate

*Examples (similar patterns, not interchangeable): Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg, Minnesota-type conditions.*

**Additional inspection focus (on top of R2):**
- Bottom seal frozen to floor / ice at threshold; water freezing under seal
- Snow or ice obstructing door path or track area
- Stiff rollers/hinges; increased noise on first cycles in cold
- Weather seal hardening, shrinkage, brittleness, gaps after thaw
- Condensation or ice on sensor lenses (verify alignment after melt)
- Opener may show strain symptoms in extreme cold—document behavior; **do not** adjust force/limits without manufacturer manual (source-required)

**Technician actions (within company policy):**
- Clear loose snow/debris from path **only if safe** and not under a compromised door
- Document seal and threshold condition; recommend seal replacement if brittle/gapped
- **Do not** force a frozen or stuck door; **do not** chip ice at bottom bracket/cable zones

**High-risk escalation:** binding after forcing; cable/spring damage; door off track; crooked door

---

#### 2. Hot / humid climate

*Examples: Florida, Texas Gulf, Southeast / Alabama-type conditions (humidity overlap possible; not identical to coastal salt-air).*

**Additional inspection focus:**
- Humidity-related corrosion on visible hardware (tracks, hinges, fasteners)—visual only
- Panel swelling/warping risk **where applicable** (material-dependent; note visual gaps/rubbing)
- Sensor lens dirt, fog, condensation, insect debris
- Seal softening, mold staining, adhesion failure at threshold
- Track/roller drag that **may be associated with** expansion or lubricant breakdown
- Opener heat stress: document symptoms only; manufacturer-specific thermal claims **source-required**

**Technician actions:**
- Clean sensor lenses per company procedure; verify alignment
- Document corrosion and seal condition; quote replacement as needed
- **No** spring/cable adjustment; corrosion at cable/spring zones → high-risk

---

#### 3. Coastal / salt-air climate

*Examples: coastal Florida, coastal Vancouver, coastal California-type environments.*

**Additional inspection focus:**
- Accelerated surface corrosion on tracks, hinges, fasteners, struts (visual)
- Rust staining, pitting, flaking paint on exposed steel
- Cable/spring **visual** corrosion or fraying—escalate; no repair steps
- Bottom fixture and bracket corrosion near tension hardware
- Increased emphasis on hardware tightness checks (non-tension fasteners per SOP)

**Technician actions:**
- Photo-document corrosion severity
- Recommend preventive maintenance frequency per company policy
- **Do not** treat rust as cosmetic only if near cables, springs, or brackets

**Regional similarity note:** Coastal salt-air ≠ inland hot-humid only; both may show corrosion but salt-air often accelerates exposed metal deterioration.

---

#### 4. Dry / dusty climate

*Examples: Arizona, Nevada, interior dry or dusty regions.*

**Additional inspection focus:**
- Dust/debris in tracks and roller paths
- Photo-eye sensor contamination or misalignment from debris
- Seal drying, cracking, UV brittleness at threshold
- Opener ventilation openings dusty (document; clean per company policy if allowed)
- Increased noise from dry roller/bearing wear

**Technician actions:**
- Remove loose debris from tracks (not tension hardware)
- Clean sensors per procedure; verify alignment
- **Do not** use compressed air near springs/cables without company safety rule

---

**Universal high-tension rule (all climates):** Springs and cables—**visual condition only**; any damage, fraying, gap in coils, or imbalance signs → stop use and schedule spring/cable service.

### Dispatcher-Safe Draft

**Opening:** “I’ll ask a few weather-related questions so we send the right technician and set expectations.”

**Climate group intake (select best fit; mixed if unsure)**

**Cold / freeze-thaw**
- Is the bottom seal frozen to the floor?
- Is there ice or snow blocking the door or threshold?
- Did the problem start on the first cold morning?
- Is the door stuck, or only the opener struggling?

**Hot / humid**
- Any sticking, swelling, or rubbing of panels?
- Rust on tracks or hardware visible?
- Sensor lights flashing or door reversing—any recent heavy rain/humidity?

**Coastal / salt-air**
- Near the ocean or bay? Visible rust on tracks, hinges, or hardware?
- Corrosion near the bottom of the door or cables (customer stays clear)?

**Dry / dusty**
- Dust storms or heavy garage dust?
- Sensors dirty or misaligned? Grinding noise from tracks?

**High-risk safety screen (all climates)**
- Broken spring or cable visible?
- Door crooked or off track?
- Door fell or someone injured?
- Customer tried to force or repair springs/cables?
- Sensors bypassed or removed?

**Scheduling:** Climate tune-up modifier + R2 scope; escalate to repair if high-risk screen positive.

### Customer-Safe Draft

**How weather can affect your garage door (simple)**

Weather does not replace a professional inspection, but it **can be associated with** common issues:

- **Cold/freezing:** Ice at the bottom seal or threshold may stick the door. **Do not force** a frozen or stuck door. Keep the path clear of ice and snow if safe to do so from outside the door path.
- **Heat and humidity:** Sticking, extra noise, or sensor problems **may occur**; humidity can contribute to wear over time. Schedule a tune-up or inspection if problems continue.
- **Coastal air:** Salt air **can accelerate** visible rust on metal parts. Rust near cables or springs is a reason to **stop using the door** and call a professional.
- **Dry and dusty:** Dust in tracks or on sensors **may affect** operation. Keep the area tidy; **do not** open or repair springs and cables yourself.

**If the door is crooked, binding, or a spring/cable looks damaged:** stop using the door, keep people and vehicles away from the door path, and schedule service with a trained garage door technician.

### Public-Safe Draft

Garage doors in different climates face different stresses: freezing thresholds in cold regions, humidity and corrosion in warm coastal areas, and dust in dry regions. Professional inspection and maintenance help catch wear early. High-tension springs and cables should only be serviced by trained technicians. Ask your local garage door company about seasonal maintenance appropriate to your area.

## Escalation Rule

**Always escalate to trained garage door technician** when:

- Spring or cable damage/corrosion suspected (visual)
- Door off track, crooked, binding, or fell
- Entrapment device failure or bypass
- Injury or property damage

**Escalate to manufacturer documentation** for opener thermal/force/travel claims.

**Escalate to expert reviewer** before approving manufacturer-specific or safety-sensitive climate claims.

**If climate profile unknown:** apply **R2 universal baseline only**; do not invent climate-specific conclusions (per Garage Door Research Question Gate).

**Never blend** climate-specific guidance with jurisdiction/code guidance in one undifferentiated approved answer.
