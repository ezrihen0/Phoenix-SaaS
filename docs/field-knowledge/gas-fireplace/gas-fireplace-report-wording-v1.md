# Gas Fireplace — Report Wording (V1)

## Purpose

Provide neutral, professional, report-safe language for job notes, inspection reports, and customer summaries.

## When to use this knowledge

- “What should I write in the report?”
- Example: “Write a report note for soot on glass.”

## Safe field checks

- Stick to observable facts: location, extent, color, pattern — avoid causal claims unless manual-backed and in scope.
- Separate customer statements from technician observations.
- Include scope limits (“visual inspection,” “maintenance per procedure”).

## What evidence to collect

- Photos referenced in note IDs if company uses media linking.
- Manual sections cited when procedure followed.

## What not to say

- In reports: “completely safe,” “code compliant,” “no venting issues” without qualified verification.
- Diagnostic certainty without evidence.
- Blame language toward customer or prior technician.

## Customer explanation language

- Reports use plain language summary; offer to walk through findings without alarming wording.

## Report-safe wording

- **Soot on glass:** “Observed soot deposit on glass panel [light/moderate/heavy]. Recommend manufacturer-specified cleaning and service inspection of combustion/venting per qualified technician if deposit recurs after service.”
- **General:** “Performed [scope] on [brand/model if recorded]. Findings: [factual list]. Limitations: [visual only / no pressure test / etc.]. Next step: [service/referral/monitor].”
- **Safety:** “Due to [finding], advised customer not to operate appliance until [qualified review/completed corrective action].”

## Escalation conditions

- Findings imply immediate hazard: report must include stop-use advisory and escalation path same day.

## Sales/service opportunity if applicable

- Note recommended optional services as “recommended” not “required” unless safety stop-use applies per protocol.
