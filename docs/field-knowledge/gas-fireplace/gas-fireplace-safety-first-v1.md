# Gas Fireplace — Safety First (V1)

## Purpose

Establish non-negotiable safety priorities before any gas fireplace service, inspection, or customer conversation. This pack is the controlling mindset for all other gas fireplace knowledge.

## When to use this knowledge

- Start of every gas fireplace visit (service, inspection, troubleshooting, sales support).
- Any mention of odor, CO concern, illness, dizziness, soot, flame irregularity, or vent blockage.
- Before discussing repairs, upgrades, or “safe to use” statements.

## Safe field checks

- Confirm the customer can describe symptoms without you diagnosing over the phone as “safe.”
- Observe appliance labeling (brand/model/serial) when accessible — do not disassemble beyond your scope.
- Look for obvious vent termination issues, blockage, damage, or improper materials (visual only).
- Note whether the appliance has been serviced per manufacturer interval (ask + visual cues only).
- If CO alarm sounded, occupants feel unwell, or strong gas odor: follow emergency protocol — evacuate, do not operate appliance, contact gas utility / emergency services per local procedure, escalate immediately.
- Document what you observed; do not promise elimination of CO risk.

## What evidence to collect

- Customer-reported symptoms (odor, headaches, alarm events) — time, duration, frequency.
- Photos of appliance nameplate, vent termination (exterior), and visible soot/glass condition (if policy allows).
- Last professional service date and who performed it (if known).
- Whether any unqualified person adjusted gas components or bypassed safeties (customer statement).

## What not to say

- “It’s definitely safe now.”
- “CO risk is eliminated after this visit.”
- “You don’t need a gasfitter.”
- Exact code compliance certification without qualified review.
- Instructions to adjust gas pressure, modify burners, bypass safeties, or alter appliance components.

## Customer explanation language

- “Gas fireplaces must be checked systematically. I’ll follow manufacturer requirements and document what we see today.”
- “If anything suggests combustion or venting concern, we stop, document, and involve a qualified gas technician per local rules.”
- “Your safety comes first — we won’t guess about gas appliances.”

## Report-safe wording

- “Visual inspection performed within scope. No combustion or venting certification provided.”
- “Customer reported [symptom]. Observed [factual visual finding]. Recommended qualified gas technician review if concerns persist.”
- “Appliance operated/stopped per procedure. Findings documented; follow manufacturer instructions for any corrective work.”

## Escalation conditions

- Gas odor, CO alarm, occupant illness, flame rollout, sustained abnormal flame, or blocked/damaged vent: **stop**, secure area, escalate to qualified gasfitter / supervisor / emergency services per company protocol.
- Missing or illegible nameplate, unknown installation, or unapproved modifications: escalate before further operation advice.
- Any task outside license/company scope: escalate.

## Sales/service opportunity if applicable

- Documented annual maintenance per manufacturer schedule (when appropriate and within scope).
- Safety inspection add-on only when framed as documented review — not as a “safety guarantee.”
- Referral to qualified gasfitter for corrective work — builds trust, reduces liability.
