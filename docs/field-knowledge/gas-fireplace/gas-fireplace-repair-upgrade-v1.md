# Gas Fireplace — Repair & Upgrade (V1)

## Purpose

Guide repair and upgrade discussions with strict compatibility and licensing boundaries.

## When to use this knowledge

- Customer asks about parts, upgrades, remote controls, new logs, glass, fans, or “make it hotter.”

## Safe field checks

- Confirm model/serial before any part discussion.
- Repairs affecting gas train, venting, or combustion: qualified gasfitter or manufacturer-authorized scope only.
- Cosmetic/accessory upgrades: verify compatibility list in manual or manufacturer parts lookup.

## What evidence to collect

- Nameplate photos, existing part numbers if visible.
- Customer desired outcome (heat, aesthetics, reliability).
- Warranty status if relevant to company policy.

## What not to say

- Recommend parts without model verification.
- “Any universal part will work.”
- Instructions to modify burners, orifices, or pressure settings.
- Promise warranty coverage without company approval.

## Customer explanation language

- “We’ll match parts to your exact model so it stays safe and covered by manufacturer rules.”
- “Some upgrades need a licensed gas technician — we’ll line that up if it’s outside today’s scope.”

## Report-safe wording

- “Discussed repair/upgrade options. Parts compatibility [verified/pending manual lookup]. Work [performed/referred/declined].”

## Escalation conditions

- Unknown model, propane conversion questions, venting changes, or high heat complaints with soot: escalate.
- Insurance/warranty claim technical opinions: supervisor.

## Sales/service opportunity if applicable

- Model-verified parts install within scope.
- Referral to qualified gasfitter for combustion or gas train work.
- Accessory sales (remote, faceplate) when compatibility confirmed.
