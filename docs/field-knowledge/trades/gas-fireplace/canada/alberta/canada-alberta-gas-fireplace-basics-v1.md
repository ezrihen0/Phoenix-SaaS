## AI-safe summary

- Treat every gas fireplace visit as **fuel-burning appliance work**: safety first, structured interview, visual inspection, evidence collection, scope-appropriate documentation.
- In Alberta, installation and alteration requirements are governed by the **Safety Codes Act** framework, declared gas codes (e.g. CSA B149 series as adopted on Alberta.ca), **manufacturer instructions**, and the **local AHJ** (municipality). Field staff must not certify code compliance or perform gas work outside licence/company scope.
- Common symptoms are triaged visually and through customer history; corrective gas component work belongs to a **qualified gasfitter** per applicable code, manual, and AHJ.
- Service and sales opportunities should be framed as documented maintenance/inspection/referral—not guarantees of safety or compliance.

## When to use this knowledge

- Alberta gas fireplace service, annual maintenance support, troubleshooting triage, customer walkthroughs, report drafting, and sales conversations within company scope.
- Before recommending operational use after concerning symptoms (odor, CO alarm, soot, vent concerns).

## Safety-first mindset

- Do not operate or advise continued use if gas odor, CO alarm, occupant illness, flame rollout, blocked/damaged vent, or strong combustion concern is reported—follow company emergency protocol and escalate.
- Follow **manufacturer installation and operation manual** for any operational steps within scope.
- Document observations; do not promise elimination of CO risk or final safety certification.

## Customer interview questions

- Appliance type as customer understands it (insert, direct vent, etc.)—verify nameplate on site.
- Symptoms: when started, frequency, triggers (weather, renovations, other appliance use).
- Fuel type (natural gas / propane) per nameplate.
- Last professional service (date, provider).
- Recent DIY work, other trades near fireplace/vent path, remote/thermostat changes.
- CO detectors present and recent test (customer report only unless testing is in scope).
- Customer goal: heat, ambiance, repair, upgrade, warranty.

## Visual inspection checklist (scope-appropriate)

- Nameplate legible: make, model, serial, input rating.
- Glass condition: soot, cracking, seal concerns (visual).
- Pilot/ignition area: visual only—no disassembly beyond scope.
- Flame appearance if safely observable per procedure (uneven, lazy, lifting—describe factually).
- Vent termination exterior: damage, blockage, improper materials, clearances **only if labeled on appliance/manufacturer instructions**—do not invent dimensions.
- Combustion air/grille areas unobstructed (visual).
- Remote/receiver location and obvious damage (visual/functional check per scope).
- Blower noise reported—listen/observe if in scope.
- Surrounding combustibles: note concerns; defer clearance rulings to manual/code/AHJ.

## Common symptoms — field triage (non-diagnostic)

| Symptom | Field notes | Typical escalation |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Black glass / soot | Document extent, recent use pattern; possible combustion/venting concern | Qualified gasfitter if persistent or with odor/CO signs |
| Pilot issue | Visual status only; do not adjust gas controls | Gasfitter |
| Remote issue | Batteries, pairing, receiver location—within scope only | Manufacturer doc / gasfitter if gas control related |
| Strange smell | Stop if gas odor; vent/construction odor vs. gas per protocol | Emergency/gas utility/gasfitter |
| Weak flame | Describe observation; no pressure adjustment | Gasfitter + manual |
| Shutdown after use | History, error codes if displayed, vent blockage visual | Gasfitter |
| Blower noise | Observe; check obstruction per manual if in scope | Service/repair per company |
| Vent / termination concern | Photo exterior termination; no clearance numbers without source | Gasfitter + AHJ if installation concern |

## Evidence to collect

- Make, model, serial from rating plate (photo if policy allows).
- Customer symptom history (timeline, frequency).
- Photos: nameplate, glass condition, exterior vent termination.
- Visible condition notes (factual, non-certifying).
- Company form completion per policy.

## Customer explanation examples

- “We’ll follow the manufacturer checklist and document what we see today—gas appliances need a systematic approach.”
- “If anything suggests venting or combustion concern, we pause and involve a qualified gas technician per Alberta requirements.”

## Report-safe wording

- “Visual inspection within scope. No gas code compliance or combustion safety certification provided.”
- “Customer reported [symptom]. Observed [factual finding]. Recommended qualified gasfitter review per manufacturer/applicable code/AHJ when [condition].”
- “Appliance labeling recorded: [make/model/serial if captured].”

## Service / sales opportunities (non-guarantee framing)

- Manufacturer-scheduled cleaning/service (when appropriate and in scope).
- Documented inspection visit.
- Blower / remote check (within scope).
- Vent termination visual review; referral for correction if concerns.
- Manufacturer manual review with customer.
- Referral to qualified gasfitter for gas connection, alteration, or code-related work.

## What AI may say

- Start with safety, customer interview, and visual inspection; collect nameplate and vent photos when appropriate.
- Describe symptoms and observations factually without diagnosing internal gas components.
- Recommend qualified gasfitter, manufacturer manual, applicable code, and local AHJ verification for installation, alteration, gas connection, or compliance questions.
- Offer maintenance/inspection services framed as documented review—not “now safe” or “code compliant.”

## What AI must not say

- Step-by-step gas pressure adjustment or burner modification instructions.
- Bypass or disable safety components.
- Appliance modification guidance.
- “This is code compliant,” “no permit needed,” or “anyone can do the gas work.”
- Exact vent or mantel clearances **unless** the user or loaded context supplies manufacturer manual or official code excerpt.
- “Service eliminates CO risk” or “definitely safe to use.”

## Escalation rule

**Stop and escalate** (qualified gasfitter, supervisor, gas utility, emergency services per company protocol) when:

- Gas odor, CO alarm, occupant illness, flame rollout, or strong combustion concern.
- Illegible/missing nameplate, unknown installation, or customer-reported unqualified gas alterations.
- Request exceeds licence/company scope.
- Customer asks for pressure adjustment, safety bypass, or compliance certification.

## Hard boundaries

- No gas pressure adjustment procedure.
- No bypassing safety components.
- No appliance modification guidance.
- No final safety/code certification.
- No promise that service eliminates CO risk.
- No exact clearances unless manufacturer manual or applicable code source is provided in context.
